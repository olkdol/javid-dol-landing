283 altcoin pumps, replayed: what helped, what it cost, and the 3 that can still liquidate

In these replays the program opens short positions after sharp rises, and a pump on a coin you are short is how that kind of account gets liquidated. We replayed 283 pumps on real prices (Sep 2024 – Sep 2026), then one real account's last three months with each candidate safeguard switched on. In the model, V19.7's default setup could still reach liquidation in 3 of the 16 largest pumps. Below: the numbers, the costs and the limits. How Part 1 was calculated
  • Universe: every USDT perpetual listed on the exchange as of September 2026, real prices from 2024-09-15 to 2026-09-15. Delisted coins are missing, so the true pump count may be higher.
  • Pump: a rise of 4× or more within 30 days during which the program's short-entry conditions switched on (the 1-hour close ** % above the Bollinger upper band and the 4-hour close above its band). The multiple is the 14-day peak over the signal price.
  • Liquidation lines: leverage 10, one add of **× at +** % from the first entry, two positions open (including the other position's loss). About 11.4× at 1/300 sizing and 18.5× at 1/500.
  • Setting comparison: every possible 5-minute entry replayed for 14 days with the exit rules (arm at ** %, trail at ** %). Funding is not included, and the live program re-enters repeatedly, so real risk can be higher.
  • Staged entry in pumps and the emergency partial close are modelled with the parameters V19.7 ships by default (checked against the code). This is a simplified model, not an engine run.

Part 2 · Designing the defense

We replayed 932 real cycles of one real, running account (13 Jun – 13 Sep 2026, 1/300 sizing) under each candidate rule, then replayed the 16 largest pumps and five coins on the program's own engine code.

What the replays showed

  1. A price stop-loss alone made the three-month result worse at every level tested. Against +1,599 USDT, results fell to between −2,393 and +371 USDT (1,228 to 3,992 lower). Tighter stops (levels 1–4) did cut the drawdown, from 74.6% to 15.6–24.2%, and left no underwater cycles, at the cost of most of the profit. Of the seven deeply underwater cycles in the sample, five later closed in profit and two were still open at the end; a stop closes that wait as a realised loss.
  2. The costly entries had one thing in common: repeat entries on the same coin. After a profitable close, the old rules shorted the same coin again with no cooldown (one coin 79 times and another 65 times in three months). The costly entries (LSK on 13 Sep, AKE at −601 USDT, RIF at −146 USDT) were all late repeat entries on one coin within a short window. A per-coin cap on re-entries alone lifted the three-month result by 30% (1,599 → 2,085 USDT) and cut the maximum drawdown from 74.6% to 60.8%, with 5 underwater cycles still left. In the LSK replay below, the cap blocked four re-entries that had closed in profit; the costly entry was handled by the stop-loss, not the cap.
  3. The combination we tested: a re-entry cap plus a stop-loss. Three-month return +31.5% (the old rules' +59.8% cut by about half), maximum drawdown 74.6% → 6.8%, underwater cycles 7 → 0, and all four months in profit (the old rules lost 198 USDT in July).
  4. The cost is return. It roughly halves; with the smaller 1/500 size on top it falls to +18.9%, about a third.

Why a position can stay open so long: by default the exit rules act on the profit side. The only loss-side action is the emergency partial close, which never fired in this sample, and the stop-loss is off. A position that never trades back below its average entry price has no other exit rule. That is why the loss side needs its own safeguards.

Read the numbers carefully. The loss side holds up: every stop fired as designed. The profit side is partly overstated: some trades (AKE +192 USDT, COAI +59 and +41, RAVE +21) opened and closed inside single one-minute wicks that a real order book may not have filled. Without them AKE and COAI end slightly negative and RAVE and ALPACA slightly positive. So the conclusion is narrow: in this replay, with the stop-loss on, the account stayed solvent through these five pumps. It does not show that pumps make money, or that V19.7 would have done the same. On the July AKE pump the real account closed a −601 USDT position (about 15% of balance); the replay's worst single loss was −35 USDT.

This engine run used V18.4.1, not V19.7. V19.7 contains these safeguards; it has not been re-run on V19.7.

What these replays do not cover

  • One account, three months, 1/300 sizing. The window (quarters of 52 and 37 pumps) did not include the busiest quarter of the two years (Q4 2025, 86 pumps), so results in calmer or wilder markets may differ.
  • The settings were chosen on the same data they are scored on. The tested combination was picked from several variants on this one sample, and LSK, the case that prompted the study, is part of it. How it behaves on new data is unknown.
  • The replay applies the re-entry lockout more strictly than the shipped build does. In the shipped build the lockout can end sooner, because the entry history it relies on is trimmed on a shorter schedule than the lockout needs. The cap's effect in the shipped build may therefore be weaker than the replay shows.
  • Stop fills are assumed at the larger of the next 5-minute open and the trigger, plus slippage. Thin order books in a pump can fill worse; one stop costs about 1% of balance, so this is unlikely to flip the results.
  • The re-entry cap is applied after the fact. In live trading another coin may have filled the blocked slot, and that result is unknown.
  • One timing difference is unexplained: the replay and the real account disagree on when one real LSK position would have closed. Stop-based results do not depend on it.
  • Four profitable months is a replay result. Leveraged futures can lose your entire capital.
V19.7

What V19.7 carries from this study

All four exchange builds (Binance, Bitget, Bybit and OKX) ship these as defaults, except the optional stop-loss. In the replays the re-entry cap alone went with a smaller drawdown (74.6% → 60.8%). Liquidation stays possible: in the model, with default settings, 3 of the 16 largest pumps could still reach it. Items marked “not tested here” are in V19.7, but this page contains no replay of them.

  • Per-coin re-entry cap. After ** entries on one coin within ** hours, further entries are blocked for a lockout period.
    On by default
  • Staged entry in pumps. When a pump is detected, the first entry is part-size and the rest waits for a higher price.
    On by default
  • Emergency partial close. Part of a position is closed when its loss reaches a set level. In the replays it fired only in the simplified pump model, never in the three-month account replay.
    On by default
  • Default size 1/500 of balance (Normal mode). 1/300 (Aggressive) can be selected.
    On by default
  • Listing-age gate. No entries on coins listed less than ** days ago.
    On by default · not tested here
  • Delisting filter. No entries or add-ons on coins announced for delisting.
    On by default · not tested here
  • Stop-loss: optional, off by default. With it off, a losing position has no price-based exit: it stays open, and can be liquidated, until the price comes back or the emergency partial close acts.
    Optional · off by default
